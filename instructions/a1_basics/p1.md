Hyperparameters, such as the learning rate or weight decay, have a major impact on the performance of
models. The sweeps below illustrate how tuning can change the apparent benefit of an intervention. But
hyperparameters are also very tricky to deal with - by their very nature, they are the parameters that we
have not yet been able to reliably automate, and some human judgment is often needed to know when
(and which) hyperparameters need to be tuned.

A key task is hyperparameter tuning. Picking the best possible choice of hyperparameters. This is often
quite complicated, due to the high-dimensionality of the hyperparameter space, and the subtle ways in
which optimal hyperparameters can change by setting. Many papers have been invalidated due to subtle
errors in baseline hyperparameter choice. Don’t let that be you!
There are a few key ideas to keep in mind. First is that some parameters are critical to re-tune and
others are often set as rough defaults. Second, when we re-tune a hyperparameter, we want to test a

Finally, many hyperparameters co-vary with each other, and you should not tune just one or the other,
but instead tune the two together. Let us now try some practical examples to see what we mean by these

(a) Start by varying a few basic hyperparameters: warmup, learning rate, batch size, and weight decay, each on their own. Many of these parameters naturally live on a log-scale, and so you will want to take the default d8 settings, a base multiplier (e.g. 3), and explore the shape of the loss as you vary the hyperparameter along this log axis (you could do 5 steps on each variable). After this investigation, you should have a good sense of what valid ranges are, what loss landscapes look like in hyperparameter space, which hyperparameters matter the most, and how you’d do a grid search.
